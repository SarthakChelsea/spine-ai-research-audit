# Week-1 Review Summary for Mihir

## Proposed central question

Which public spine-imaging datasets and pretrained AI models are actually accessible, technically reusable, and clinically fit for specified imaging, pain, treatment, and complementary/integrative-care research questions?

## Proposed differentiating contribution

The review will not only catalogue resources. It will determine whether individual imaging data can be linked to pain, function, psychiatric/biopsychosocial context, medications, treatments, treatment timing, response, complications, and longitudinal follow-up. It will explicitly quantify absent information.

## Proposed clinical conclusion framework

Many resources may be fit for segmentation, localization, or image-label tasks, while not supporting pain phenotyping, prognosis, treatment selection, treatment-response prediction, or integrative-care research. This distinction is based on required data elements, not the overall size or technical quality of a dataset.

## Decisions requested

1. Confirm which pain measures are sufficient for pain phenotyping.
2. Confirm mandatory versus desirable psychiatric/biopsychosocial variables.
3. Confirm the working definition of complementary/integrative treatment.
4. Decide whether physical therapy should be treated as rehabilitation, integrative care, or both with separate tags.
5. Confirm minimum requirements for treatment-response and observational treatment-selection fitness.

## Included infrastructure

- protocol and eligibility criteria;
- student screening/extraction manual;
- detailed pain/treatment/integrative-care codebook;
- dataset and model registry templates;
- clinical fitness matrix;
- technical access/reproducibility audit forms;
- label ontology template;
- ten-resource pilot plan; and
- initial verified seed-paper tracker.
